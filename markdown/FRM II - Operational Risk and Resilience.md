## A. Holistic Overview of Operational Risk and Resilience
### <!-- C1 p19 --> Introduction to Operational Risk and Resilience
#### Definition of Operational Risk by BCBS
Basel Committee On Banking Supervision (BCBS)

Risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed processes, people and systems or from external events (PPSE).
The definition includes legal risk, but excludes strategic and reputational risk.
Where approprite, strategic and reputational risk should be considered as operational risk.

##### Categories of Operational Risk Events under Basel II
* Internal fraud (IF)
  unauthorized activities, theft and fraud, embezzlement, misappropriation of assets, bribe
* External fraud (EF)
  theft and fraud (check kitting and forgery), systems security (hacker attack)
* Employment practices and workplace safety (EPWS)
  more prominent in America, there is more a cultrue of litigation against the employers
  * employee relations
  * safe enviroment
  * diversity and discrimination
* Clients, products, and business practices (CPBP)
  disputes with clients, regulatory fines from improper business practices
  client misinformation, complaints an discounts due to errors, product misspecification
* Damage to physical assets (DPA)
  natural disaster and terrorism
  very few firms collect loss on this rick type, as these are usually too small or too large. Senario analysis and insurance
* Business disruption and system failures (BDSF)
  IT breakdown, outages (electricity)
* Execution, delivery, and process management (EDPM)
  failed transaction processing: processing errors, missing documentation, vendor disputes

##### Frequency and Severity of Seven Events
CPBP consititues more than half of loss from less than one quarter of frequency.
EF, EPWS, CPBP, EDPM contributes most of risk events.
CPBP and EDPM are to explain most of the loss.
just direct financial losses, no reputational damage, market capitalization, the latter far exceeds the former.

#### Characteristic of Operational Risk ***
* heterogenous: different
* idiosyncratic and diffuse: could not be totally hedged
* heavy tailed: large number of small loss and small number of large loss
* interconnected: share common internal cause (weak internal control etc.)
* dynamic: operational risk exposure evolve with business activities


#### Operational Risk Management (ORM) Frameworks
* governance (assignment of rols and responsibilities)
* risk identification → risk appetite → risk assessment → risk mitigation → risk monitoring
* conduct and culture
ORM cycle: risk identification → risk assessment → risk mitigation → risk monitoring
risk appetite is determined by board of directors

####  Operational Resilience
ability to recover from operational disruptions

##### OR in UK
the ability to prevent, adapt to, respond to, recover from, and learn from operational disruptions

* amount of disruption that could be tolerated
* must contain a time-based metric, similar to recovery time

##### OR in USA
ORM is central component

Operational resilience has 2 critical supporting pillar: business continuity management, IT systems resilience

##### KPIs, KRIs and KCIs (***)
###### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
error rates on retail transactions

###### Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)
examples of KRIs measuring likelihood:
* increase in number of trasactions per staff
* drop in customer satisfaction scores (client attrition)
* increase in level of sales required to achieve a performance objective (fraud)

examples of KRIs measuring impact: 
* increase in level of proprietary company knowledge held by a key employee
* increase in sensitivity of data held on a given server
* increase in value generated by top-10 clients

###### Key Control Indicators (KCIs)
* missed due diligence items
* lack of segregation of duties
* overlooked errors in four-eye checks (reviews by two competent individuals)

Three indicators overlap, the failure of a control function is jointly KPI, KRI, KCI.

#### Quantitative Risk Assessment (***)
##### Causal Analysis of Scenarios

##### Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)

##### Swiss Cheese: Control Layering
cumulative act model, each defensive layer is like a slice of Swiss cheeses.
The presence of one hole causes bad outcomes when holes in many layers momentarily line up.
assessing independence of controls is at least important as assessing reliability of each individual control.

#### Operational Risk Modeling and Capital (***)
loss distribution approach (LDA): Advanced Measurement Approach (AMA)
* frequency: modeled with Poisson and negative binomial (probability of k failures before r sucesses)
* severity: lognormal, Weibull and Generalized Pareto (GPD, more heavy tailed)

Severity and frequency are assumed to be independent, and convoluted into an aggregated loss distribution by Monte Carlo.
mix internal and external data: scale, cut-off mix (threshold), filter
losses in each risk classes are not iid.

#### Internal Controls: Types and Testing
##### Internal Control Types (***)
* preventive controls: reduce likelihood of an incident
  * segregation of duties
  * access controls
  * levels of authorization
  * process automation
* detective controls: 
  * exception report
  * file reconciliation
  * smoke detector
  * intrusion detection system
* corrective controls:
  * data back-ups
  * IT systems patch management
  * continuity planning 
  * systems redundancy
* directive controls: rules and prescriptions
  * procedures and guidance manuals
  * internal training 
  * team supervision
  * work handover procedures

##### Control Design
weakly designed control: 
* optimistic: sign-off for large volume of documents before a deadline
* collective: "four-eyes check" diluting accountability
* more of the same: responding to a failure of collective controls by adding more controllers

##### Control Testing (***)
* self-certification or inquiry
  limited to secondary controls related to low risk environments
* examination: supporting evidence and documentation
  moderate assurance and suited for automated controls and sampling of manual controls
* observation: oversight
* reperformance (穿行测试)
  strongest form of testing

#### Risk Mitigation by Process Design
prevention through design, examples are checklists, standardization, optimized environments or system design

##### Typology and Mitigation of Human Error (***)
* skilled-based: slip
  inattention, distraction, environment, tiredness
  controls: adjust pace, improve environment, re-engineer process
* rule-based: flawed rules
  flawed products, misleading instruction, wrong incentives
* knowledge-based:
  controls: training, documented procedures, help file, escalation rules
* violation: voluntary misdeed
  controls: supervisory control, improve culture which reward adherence to rules

#### Risk Capital
The objective of BCBS prudential regulation is threefold:
1. To ensure the solvency and soundness of all financial intermediaries
2. To provide customers protection from undue risks (failure, fraud, opportunistic behavior)
3. To promote the efficient and competitive performance of financial instituions

Objective 1 is primary objective, addressed through regulatory capital requirements
Objective 2 and 3 are attained through senior management's competence

In U.S., the focus shifted from capital estimation to stress testing.
Capital is through-the-cycle concept, while stress testing is point-in-time.

Economic Capital: linked to credit rating, influencing cost of funds.

##### Risk Aggregation for Capital Assessment (*)
intra-risk diversification: diversification within each risk class
operational risk can bring important diversification benefits to capital, because it has low correlation to other financial risks.

#### Stress Testing (*)
##### Taxonomy
* dimension 1
  * quantitative: sensitivity model to parameter shocks
  * qualitative: scenario analysis such as macro stress testing (multiple parameters), reverse stress testing
* dimension 2 
  * measurable: probabilistic
  * immeasurable: possibilistic

Globally systemically important banks (G-SIBs) are provided a list of macro shock to assess the solvency level.

##### Reverse Stress Testing
Find how bad an adverse scenario would need to be to achieve a pre-defined outcome, analyze immeasurable risks.
apt for assessing operational resilience

##### Operational Risk Stress Testing Models
severity can be more complex to model than frequency because of being highly impacted by tail events.
