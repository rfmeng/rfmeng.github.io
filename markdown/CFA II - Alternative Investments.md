### <!-- C1 p171 --> Introduction to Commodities and Commodity Derivatives
#### Physical Commodity Markets
##### Commodity Sectors
considerations in fundamental analysis:
* production and inventory
* stock and flow method (存量和流量)
* timing: seasonality, shape of commodity futures curve

Sectors:
* energy
* grains: corn, soy, wheat and rice
* metals: aluminum, nickel, zinc, lead, tin and iron
* precious metal: gold, silver and platinum
* softs (cash crops): cotton, cocoa, sugar and coffee

##### Life Cycle of Commodities
a short life cycle allows for rapid adjustment to outside events
* industrial/previous metals
  * most flexible life cycle
  * difficult for suppliers to halt production
* softs
  * robusta and arabica coffee

##### Valuation of Commodities
* tangible items has intrinsic economic value
* transportation and storage costs
* roll return (展期)
* physical delivery and cash settlement
  * some participants do not have the ability to physical delivery, so the price difference may exist

#### Commodities Futures Markets
##### Futures Market Participants
* hedger
* informed investors
* liquidity providers
* arbitrageurs
* commodity exchanges
* commodity market analysts
* commodity regulators
  * International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO)

##### Spot and Futures Pricing
contango: future price higher
* word of contango comes from the continuation, the buyer pays contango fee to make delivery
backwardation: spot price higher
calendar spread (basis, 基差): spot price - futures price 

##### Theories of Future Returns
* Insurance theory
  * commodity producer short futures to remove price volatility, resulting in backwardation
  * does not explain existence of contango
* Hedging pressure hypothesis
  * both producers and consumers have hedging pressure
* Theory of storage
  * future price = spot price + storage cost - convenience yield
  * convenience yield: holding commodities acts as a supply buffer

##### Component of Future Returns
* price return (spot yield)
* collateral return
  * yield from the margin (bond or cash equivalents)
* roll return: (near price - far price) / near price * roll portion
  * eg. accounting caculation for commodity index
* rebalance return: for commodity index

#### Commodity Swaps
##### Characteristics
* risk management without the need to set up futures contract
* customization

##### Classification
* excess return swap: net change in prices of futures, actually a series of European options
* total return swap: all return
* basis swap: swap on spread
* variance swap
* volatility swap

#### Commodity Indexes
##### Primary Roles
##### Characteristics
* breadth of coverage (commodity sectors)
* weightings
* rolling methodology
* rebalancing

##### Major Commodity indexes
* S&P GSCI (S&P Goldman Sachs Commodity Index): production value weighting
* Thomson Reuters/CoreCommodity CRB Index: fixed weights

##### Rebalancing Frequency
Frequent rebalancing can lead to underperformance in a trending market.
CRB and RICI is monthly rebalanced while others are annually rebalanced

### <!-- C3 p275 --> Investments in Real Estate through Publicly Traded Securities
#### Types of Publicly Traded Real Estate Securities
##### Types
* REITs: tax-advantaged entities operate real estate property
  * usually with premium
* MBS
* REOCs

##### ! REIT Structures
* distribute 90%-100% of taxable earnings
* invest at least 75% of their assets in real estate
* derive at least 75% of income from rental or interest mortgages
* at least 100 shareholders and no fewer than 5 can own more than 50% of the shares (the five-or-fewer rule, 6 is allowed)

fully integrated REITs have fewer conflicts than REITs that are externally advised or managed
shareholder of REIT dividends:
* portion of rent is classified as ordinary income
* REIT distributions of capital gains taxed at capital gain tax rate
* foreign invesotrs are subject to the host country's withholding (预缴税款) tax

##### Market Size
In north America, REITs takes 95% of total market, against Non-REITs and REOCs.

##### Benefits and Disadvantages
disadvantages:
* lack of retained earnings
* constrained in the types of assets they own

#### Net Asset Value Approach
##### Accounting for Investment Properties
IFRS: cost model or fair value model
GAAP: cost model

##### Net Asset Value per Share
capitalizing the rental streams (NOI) should be adjusted to reflect a full year's rent
estimated operating value = NOI / cap rate
intangible assets (goodwill, deferred tax asset and liabilities) are excluded
application:
* discount rate used by private owner could be different from cap rate
* ignoring intangible assets
* NAV estimates could be subjective when market is illiquid or when REITs own hundreds of properties (hard to find true cap rate)
* if REIT is performing well, REITs and REOCs should trade at premiums
* stock has liquidity and thus has lower return


#### Relative Value Approach
##### Relative Value Approach to Value REIT Stocks
P/FFO, P/AFFO, EV/EBITDA

##### FFO and AFFO
* Funds from Operations (FFO):
  * FFO = net income - net gain from sales of properties + depreciation and amortization
* Adjusted Funds from Operations (AFFO):
  * AFFO = FFO - noncash rent adjustment - recurring maintainance capital expenditures
  * also Funds Available for Distribution (FAD) or Cash Available for Distribution (CAD)


##### P/FFO and P/AFFO
drawbacks:
* land parcels and empty buildings may not have FFO but have value

#### Private vs. Public

  * potential inflation hedge

  * tax benefits (accelerated depreciation)

  * track real estate fundamentals over the long term

### <!-- C4 p319 --> Hedge Fund Strategies
#### Introduction and Classification
##### Introduction
* Pros:
  * a key way to access the best investment talent
  * alpha
* Cons:
  * high fees
  * complex memorandum documentation
  * lack of transparency

liquid alternatives: mutual fund, closed-end fund (封闭式基金), ETF
* liquid altternatives significantly underperform similar strategy hedge funds

##### Classification
* Equity Strategies
  * long/short equity: mostly long
  * dedicated short and short-biased: mostly short
  * equity market neutral
* Event-Driven Strategies
  * merger arbitrage
  * distressed securities
* Relative Value Strategies: contains all long and short strategy
  * fixed income arbitrage
  * convertible bond arbitrage
* Opportunistic Strategies: top-down approach, macro-oriented
  * global macro
  * managed futures
* Specialist Strategies
  * volatility strategies
  * reinsurance strategies
* Multi-Manager Strategies
  * multi-strategy
  * fund-of-fund


#### Strategies
##### Long/Short Strategies
long the undervalued and short the overvalued, also relative value strategy
goal is to find more sources of idiosyncratic alpha
return profile: roughly long-only approach return but with much lower std
leverage usage: more market-neutral, more levered
* lever: gross exposure / principal

types of fund managers:
* Specialist L/S: fundamental analysis, focus on specific sectors
* Generalist L/S: less sufficient alpha relative to their fees

##### Dedicated Short Selling and Short-Biased
dedicated short selling: short only
short-biased: short stock and long index
uptick rule: when a stock decreaes by 10%, a short order can only be filled at a price higher than the last price.
risk profile:
* typically handled in a limited partnership
* low leverage

implementation:
* monitoring CDS spread, put options
* protective put: holding stock and long put

##### Equity Market Neutral
examples:
* pairs trading
* stub trading
  * A owns 90% and 70% of B and C, A overvalued, B and C undervalued, thus short 1 A long 0.9 B and 0.7 C
* multi-class trading: buy A share sell B share (non-voting)

risk profile:
* prefered replacements for fixed income managers
* more useful during non-tranding or declining markets
* modest return and high level of diversification
* often seek for high leverage because of beta risks are hedged away

##### Merger Arbitrager
types:
* soft-catalyst: anticipation of an event to occur
* hard-catalyst: in reaction to announced event where security prices have not fully converged

investment characteristics:
* for cash-for-stock acquisition, buy the target company
* for stock-for-stock acquisition, buy the target and sell the acquiring company
* usually target company stock price increases and acquirer stock price decreases
* riskless bond and a short put (on acquisition)
* owning an additional call if another acquirer makes a higher bid

risk profile:
* deals more like to fail in market stress periods
* cross-border merger and acquisition have higher risk
* low return and mid std
* high leverage
* CDS could be sold on the target company (if acquirer has superior credit), or bought on target company as hedge


##### Distressed Securities
capital structure arbitrage: long securities expecting to receive acceptable recoveries 
risk profile:
* higher return and more variability
* distressed securities are illiquid, pricing may mark to model with return smoothing
* return tend to be lumpy
* moderate or low leverage because of higher volatility

Implementation:
* liquidation situation
  * buy bond at a discount and short stock: expecting fully payment of principal
* reorganization situation
* fulcrum (支点) securities: manager buy debt and swap for new shares (existing debt and equity are cancelled) if the company's operating prospects are still good

##### Fixed-Income Arbitrage
immunize interest rate risk by taking duration-neutral positions
risk profile:
* high leverage

implementation:
* yield curve trade: long and short at different points on the yield curve
* carry trade: long a higher yielding security and short a lower yielding

##### Convertible Bond Arbitrage
the embedded options tend to trade at low volatility, the key is to extract cheap embedded optionality
risk profile:
* high leverage
* Convertible Arbitrage Indices

##### Global Macro
multiple asset classes and derivatives, to predict the trend
top-down and fundamental analysis

##### Managed Futures
natural positive skewness: profit time low but high profit each time
implementation:
* time-series momentum: net long or net short
* cross-sectional momentum: market-neutral position

##### Volatility Trading
most liquid volatility contracts: short-term VIX
risk profile:
* source of alpha
* OTC is structured under ISDA documentation

##### Reinsurance/Life Settlements
life settlements:
* view of life expectancy, uncorrelated alpha
* designated insured persion is likely to die earlier
* buy the insurance from the policyholder, pay the premium and expect to receive death benefit

reinsurance:
* buy from secondary market
* diversity in terms of geographic exposure
* sufficient buffer in terms of loan loss reserve (insurance company's reserve to make a payment firstly and then hedge fund)
* high premium

##### Fund-of-Funds
* pros:
  * currency hedging capabilities
  * economies of sacle
* cons:
  * double layer of fees
  * inability to net performance fees on individual managers
  * additional principal agent relationship
  * lack of transparency into individual hedge fund

characteristics:
* lower investment threshold
* endowments, foundations and pension plans turn to FOFs because of regulation
* commercial bank loans
* extract fee break, liquidity term, added transparency provisions from the underlying hedge fund

##### Multi-Strategy Hedge Funds
* due on the total performance after netting
* "Pass-through" fee model: costs of paying individual teams (compared to FoFs) and incentive fee 
* outperformed FoFs, more volatile
* higher leverage


example:
* A charges 1% management fee and 10% incentive fee, invests 50% and 50% into two funds B and C, each charging a 2% management fee and a 20% incentive fee (net of management fee)
  B earns gross return 20% and C loses 5%
  * for B: incentive fee = 20% × (20%-2%) = 3.6%, net of fees return = 14.4%
  * for C: net of fees return = -7%
  * for A: gross return = 50% × 14.4% - 50% × 7% = 3.7%, management fee = 10% × (3.7% - 1%) = 0.27%, net of fees return = 2.43%

##### ! Analysis
###### Conditional Factor Risk Model
multi-factor model, using a conditional linear factor model
return = alpha + beta × factor + D × beta × factor
* D: dummy variable that equals 1 during financial crisis periods

risk factors:
* equity risk: S&P 500
* Interest rate risk
* currency risk
* commodity risk
* credit risk
* volatility risk: VIX

for highly correlated risk factors, keep the one with higher adjusted $R^2$
