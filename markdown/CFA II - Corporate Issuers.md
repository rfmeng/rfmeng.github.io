### <!-- C1 p11 --> Analysis of Dividends and Share Repurchases
#### Concept of Dividends and Dividend Policies
##### Dividends
###### Forms of Dividends
* cash dividend
  * regular
  * special
  * liquidating
* stock dividend
* stock split (reverse stock split)
  * 2-for-1 stock split
  * 1-for-20 reverse stock split (并股)

###### Effects on Shareholders' Wealth
wealth of shareholders will not change if no tax

###### Effects on Financial Ratios
cash dividend:
* reduce asset and equity
* liquidity ratio will decrease (CA/CL)
* financial leverage ratio will increase (D/E, D/A)
* EPS remain the same

stock dividend and stock split:
* share price and EPS will decline

##### Dividend Policies
* Dividend policy does not matter (MM)
* Dividend policy does matter
  * bird-in-hand argument: more portion of company is transfered to shareholder
  * tax argument: dividend tax and capital gain tax (sell stocks) have difference
* other theoretical issues
  * information signaling: positive information
  * agency cost
    * between shareholders and managers: reduce free cash flow for managers to invest and run out of control
    * between shareholders and bondholders: dividend transfer wealth from bondholders to shareholders

#### Dividends Payout Policies
##### Factors Affecting Dividend Policy
Tax considerations: 
* double taxation system 
  * dividend = $\text{EBT}\times\text{payout\%}(1-t_\text{{corsp}})(1-t_\text{{div}})$
  * effective tax rate = $1-(1-t_\text{{corp}})(1-t_\text{{div}})$
* dividend imputation tax system: total tax = shareholder tax rate
  * dividend = $\text{EBT}\times\text{payout\%}(1-t_\text{{div}})$
  * effective tax rate = shareholder tax rate
* split-rate tax system
  * similar to double taxation system, but $t_\text{corporate on dividend}<t_\text{corp}$
  * dividend = $\text{EBT}\times\text{payout\%}(1-t_\text{{corpdiv}})(1-t_\text{{div}})$
  * effective tax rate = $1-(1-t_\text{{corpdiv}})(1-t_\text{{div}})$ 


##### Dividends Payout Policies
###### 1. Stable Dividend Policy
expected dividend = previous dividend + (expected earnings × target payout ratio - previous dividend) × adjustment factor (1/N, N is the number of years over which adjustment should take place)

###### 2. Constant Dividend Payout Ratio Policy
payout ratio = dividend/net income = DPS (dividend per share)/EPS

#### Share Repurchase and its Effects
##### Share Repurchase Methods
treasury shares (库存股, provision account for equity): repurchased shares, not considered for dividends, voting or computing EPS
* buy in the open market
* tender offer (邀约收购)
  * fixed price tender offer, typically at a premium
  * Dutch auction, to determine the lowest price
* direct negotiation, typically at a premium

##### Financial Statement Effects of Repurchase
* repurchase with cash:
  * asset and equity will decline, financial leverage will increase
  * EPS will increase
* repurchase with debt:
  * debt increase and equity decline, financial leverage will increase
  * (?) if earning yield (E/P) > after-tax cost of debt, EPS will increase
    * (-) suppose total earning (net profit) is $E$, the number of outstanding share is $N$, stock price is $P$, the principal of the debt is $L$ and the after-tax cost is $i$. 
    * $\frac{E}{S}?\frac{E-iL}{S-\frac{L}{P}}\Leftrightarrow iSL?\frac{EL}{P}\Leftrightarrow i?\frac{E}{SP}$

effect on BVPS:
* Book Value Per Share (BVPS) = book value of equity / shares outstanding
* if repurchase price > original BVPS, decrease

#### Analysis of Dividend and Share Repurchase
##### Dividends vs. Repurchase
If the tax treatment of two methods is the same, share repurchase equivalent to dividend
share repurchase wins in aspects of:
* potential tax advantages
* managerial flexibility (not restricted to periodically dividend)
* offseting dilution from employee stock options
* increasing financial leverage
* increasing EPS

distributing cash that are not expected to continue in the future years:
* special cash dividend equivalent to repurchase


##### Analysis of Dividend Safety
* Dividend payout ratio = $\frac{\text{dividend}}{\text{net income}}$
* Dividend coverage ratio = $\frac{\text{net income}}{\text{dividend}}$
* FCFE coverage ratio = $\frac{\text{FCFE}}{\text{dividend + share repurchase}}$
  * FCFE: Free Cash Flow to Equity
  * FCFE = Cashflow from operating - Investment + Net borrowing

### <!-- C3 p111 --> Cost of Capital: Advanced Topics
$\text{WACC}=w_dr_d(1-t)+w_pr_p+w_er_e$
* $r_d=r_f+\text{credit spread}$
* $r_p$ is prefered stock
* $r_e=r_f+\text{ERP}+\text{IRP}$: CAPM
  * ERP: Equity Risk Premium
  * IRP: Idiosyncratic Risk Premium
* $w$: long-term target capital structure

#### Cost of Capital Factors
|Top-down, External|Bottom-up, Company specific|
|:-:|:-:|
|capital availability|revenue, earnings and cashflow volatility|
|market conditions|asset nature and liquidity|
|legal and regulatory considerations, country risk|financial stength, profitability and leverage|
|tax jurisdictiion|security features|

* market conditions:
  * higher inflation rate (price level increasing): higher benchmark interest rates
  * recession: higher credit spread
  * expansion: lower credit spread
  * lower interest/exchange rates volatility: lower WACC
* greater invesotr protection: lower credit spread
* higher marginal income tax rate: lower WACC


* revenue:
  * higher stability in sales: lower
  * higher revenue concentration: higher
  * higher ESG risk: higher
* asset:
  * higher portion of fungible and tangible assets: lower
* financial strength
  * higher interest coverage (EBIT/interest): lower
* securities features
  * debt:
    * callability (发行赎回): higher
    * putability: lower
    * convertibility: lower
  * prefered stock cumulative: lower
    the should have been paid dividend is cumulative, because company may not pay out dividend if net income is negative
  * common stock inferior cash flow/voting rights: higher

#### Cost of Debt
##### Cost of Debt Estimation
* traded debt: YTM
* non-traded debt: 
  * with credit rating: similar bond yield
  * without credit rating: synthetic credit ratings 

##### Bank Loans
amortizing loans typically have a lower cost than non-amortizing loans

##### Leases
* operating lease: pay rent
* finance lease: buy the equipment

Rate Implicit in the Lease (RIIL): present value of lease payments + present value of residual value to lessor = fair value of leased asset + lessor's direct initial costs
Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR): rate of a collateralized loan over the same term


#### Equity Risk Premium
##### Estimating ERP
###### Historical Approach
historical $R_f$ and $R_M$
key decision:
* equity index: broad based
* time period
  * longer period is more stable but not representative of the current market
* mean type
  * arithmetic mean: sensitive to extreme value, overestimates expected terminal value of wealth
  * geometric mean: gives outlier less weight, estimates expected terminal value of wealth
* risk-free rate proxy
  * government bond YTM: more closely match infinite equity duration, but not completely risk-free
  * government bill rate: exact estimate risk-free rate but not close match infinite equity duration

survivorship bias tends to inflate historical estimates of ERP

###### Forward-Looking Approach
* survey-based estimates
  * ask experts what they expect
  * sensitive to recent market returns
* dividend discount models
  * Gordon Growth Model, $P_0=\sum\frac{D_0(1+g)^i}{(1+r)^i}=\frac{D_1}{r-g}$, see CFA I - Equity Investments (2)
    $r_e = \frac{D_1}{P_0}+g$, $\text{ERP} =\frac{D_1}{P_0}+g-r_f$ 
  * multiple earnings growth stages: fast growth, transition growth, mature growth
* macroeconomic models
  * $E(R_e)=dy+\Delta\frac{P}{E}+i+g+\Delta S$
  * $\text{ERP} = dy+\Delta\frac{P}{E}+i+g-\Delta S-r_f$
  * expected infaltion: Treasury Inflation Protected Security (TIPS)
    $\text{YTM}_{\text{Treasury bond}}-\text{YTM}_{\text{TIPS}}$
  * reliable in developed markets

#### Cost of Equity
##### DDM
$P_0=\sum\frac{D_t}{(1+r_e)^t}+\frac{P_n}{(1+r_e)^t}$

##### Bond Yield plus Risk Premium Approach
$r_e=r_d+RP$, RP can be estimated using historical mean difference in returns between equity market index and corporate bond index
* no prescription of which traded debt to select

##### Risk Based Models
CAPM
Fama-French: $r_e=r_f+\beta_1\text{ERP}+\beta_2\text{SMB}+\beta_3\text{HML}+\beta_4\text{RMW}+\beta_5\text{CMA}$
* SMB: small minus big, size premium
* HML: book-to-market ratio high minus low, value premium
* RMW: robust minus weak, profitability premium
* CMA: conservative minus aggresive, investment premium
  * conservative: low asset growth rate

##### Cost of Equity for Private Companies
###### Expanded CAPM
$r_e=r_f+\beta_\text{peer}\text{ERP}+\text{SP}+\text{IP}+\text{SCRP}$
* SP: size premium
* IP: industry risk premium
* SCRP: specific-company risk premium



###### Build-up Approach
$r_e=r_f+\text{ERP}+\text{SP}+\text{SCRP}$
* ERP: average risk large-cap public equity

##### Country Spread Models
* Country Risk Premium $\text{CRP}=\text{sovereign yield spread}\times\frac{\sigma_\text{equity}}{\sigma_\text{bond}}$
  $\text{ERP for emerging}= \text{ERP for developed}+\lambda\text{CRP}$
  * CRP: country risk premium
  * $\lambda$: exposure of country risk premium
* Global CAPM (GCAPM)
  * single factor for global market index
  * assuming no significant risk differences across countries
* International CAPM (ICAPM)
  $r_e=r_f+\beta_G(r_{gm}-r_f)+\beta_C(r_c-r_f)$
  * $r_{gm}$: global market index
  * $r_c$: foreign currency index


### <!-- C4 p165 --> Capital Restructuring
#### Corporate Evolution, Actions, and Motivations
types of corporate structural changes:
* investment: increase the size or scope of company
  Motivations:
  * cost and revenue synergies
  * improve capabilities or secure resources
  * acquire undervalued targets
* devestment
  Motivations:
  * conglomerate discount (多元化折价): lack of concentration, friction cost
  * liquidity needs
  * regulatory requirements
* restructuring: improve the cost and financing structure without altering the size or scope
  * improve return on capital
    * opportunistic improvement (active)
    * forced improvement
  * financial challenges, including bankrupcy and liquidation


types of corporate restucturing
* investment
  * equity investment
  * joint venture
  * acquisition
* devestment
  * sales/divestiture: reallocate capital
  * spin off: separate a distinct part of business into a new and independent company
    * remove incompatibilities (like different price and quality target)
* restructuring
  * forced improvement
    * cost restructuring: outsourcing and offshoring (离岸外包)
    * balance sheet restructuring: change asset or capital structure
      * sale leaseback (售后回租): sell PPE and rent back
      * dividend recapitalization: borrow money and buyback
    * reorganization (重整): court-supervised restructuring process
  * opportunistic improvement
    * alter the business model
      * franchising: divest asset and license intellectual property (IP) to third-party
    * cost restructuring
    * balance sheet restructuring
* leveraged buyout: borrow a significant amount of money to exit

#### Evaluating Corporate Restructurings
##### Initial Evaluation
material: value of acquisition exceeds 10% of the acquirer's enterprise value

##### Preliminary Valuation
###### 1. Comparable Company Analysis
mean of valuation multiples of similar listed companies to value a target company
* enterprice multiples: EV/EBITDA, EV/sales
* earning multiples: P/E, P/sales

disadvantage:
* comparable companies may not exist
* sensitive to market mispricing
* takeover premium must be estimated

###### 2. Comparable Transaction Analysis
similar to comparable company analysis
advantages:
* takeover premium is embedded in multiples

disadvantages:
* past acquirers might be overpaid or underpaid
* comparable transactions may not exist
* necessary to make adjustments to reflect changes in conditions

###### 3. Premium Paid Analysis
$\text{PRM}=\frac{\text{DP}-\text{SP}}{\text{SP}}$
* PRM: takeover premium
* DP: Deal Price per share
* SP: unaffected (before announcement) Stock Price per share
